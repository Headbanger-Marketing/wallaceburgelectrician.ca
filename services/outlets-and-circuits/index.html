An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Wallaceburg property

List what will plug in at each new position, and note which existing receptacles have stopped working or are on a breaker that trips. Photograph the walls and mark any surface you cannot allow to be damaged.

Ask whether the new receptacles will share an existing circuit or need a new one, and how the cable will be routed with the least wall opening. Ask who repairs and finishes any opening.

What should the assessment establish?

A finished room at a Wallaceburg address is getting more receptacles, with as little wall opening as possible. The contractor traces where cable can reach without major demolition and marks where it cannot. Where a receptacle in the room already fails or trips, the report explains whether its cause lies inside the property, before more are added. Whether the new positions can share the existing circuit or need their own is a finding of the visit. Any concealed route that could not be examined is noted, and the scope lists each position, the surfaces to be opened and who repairs them.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can extra plug-in points go into a room with finished drywall and paint?

Cable can sometimes follow baseboards or reach the room from an accessible space, but the contractor must see the room first. Ask for the routing plan and for repairs to be listed in the scope.

Will new receptacles make the breaker trip more often?

That depends on what plugs in and whether the new positions share a circuit with existing equipment. Tell the contractor about every device you expect to run together, and note which breaker serves the room.
