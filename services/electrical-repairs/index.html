A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Wallaceburg property

Write down which rooms, floors or outbuildings are without power, when it began and what was running. Note whether neighbours, street lights or a meter display show anything unusual, and do not keep resetting a breaker that trips again.

Ask the contractor how the visit will separate a utility supply issue from a fault inside the property, and what written finding you will receive. Ask also who should be contacted first if the supply side appears responsible.

What should the assessment establish?

The cause of a dark area or outbuilding lies either on the utility side or on the property side. If a fault inside the property is found, the scope names the cause, the repair proposed and any areas that must be opened; if the utility side is responsible, the finding says that instead. The finding is in writing, so the owner of the Wallaceburg property can share it with the utility. The contractor describes what was observed and which part of the installation was reviewed, without guessing from the age of the equipment. Anything untested or hidden is listed too.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Why did one outbuilding lose power while the house is fine?

The cause may be a fault on the feed to that building or a protective device that tripped, but only a contractor can say which. Do not reset it repeatedly, and describe what was running beforehand.

What should I record if the power returns before anyone visits?

Write down the time it went off, how long it lasted and what was running, and photograph the panel door from outside. A contractor can use that history to judge whether the pattern points to the supply or the property.
