Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your Wallaceburg property

Sketch the hallway and stair in words, marking each switch, each light and the dark spots at night. Note any light that flickers or dims, and whether it happens on its own or only when other equipment starts.

Ask how the contractor will tell a fixture or control problem from a wiring or supply one before wiring is changed, and how finished ceilings above a stair would be opened and restored.

What should the assessment establish?

Where do the new hallway and stair lights and their switches go, and how does cable reach them? The scope lists each fixture, each switch position and the surfaces to be opened, with the name of whoever restores them. Do the existing wiring and supports suit the chosen fixtures and controls? The contractor gives a finding on that. What lies behind any flicker or dimming that the Wallaceburg hallway or stair shows today? The report ties it to the fixture, the control or the wiring where it relates to one. Lights that remain usable at night during the work are named too.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can stair lights be added without opening the finished ceiling?

Sometimes the route can be planned through existing openings or accessible spaces, but that is for the contractor to judge on site. Ask which surfaces would be opened and who repairs and paints them.

Is a flickering hallway light a bulb problem or a supply problem?

It can come from the bulb, the control, the fixture, the wiring or the supply. Note when it flickers and whether other lights do the same, then ask a contractor to assess it rather than swapping parts repeatedly.
