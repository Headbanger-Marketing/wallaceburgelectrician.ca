Before work begins

Sort the supply event from the inside fault first.

Is your home a bungalow or split-level with a finished basement? Its panel may be in a utility room, behind storage or inside a finished space, and lower-floor cables can run through closed ceilings and walls. Other layouts can hide them too.

Before you call anyone, note whether neighbours or street lights also lost power, and which rooms or buildings on the property went dark. That detail helps decide whether the first call goes to the utility or to a contractor.

What should two quotes say about whether a problem is inside the home?

Each quote should state what was examined and whether the contractor found a cause on the property side or suspects the supply. If one quote proposes replacement without naming a cause, ask for the reasoning in writing before you compare prices.

Who should I contact first when one area of the house loses power?

If the whole street is affected, the utility is the usual first contact. If only one room, floor or outbuilding is dark while neighbours have power, a Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess the property side.

Should I keep resetting a breaker that trips again straight away?

No. Repeated resetting may obscure the pattern a contractor needs. Leave the device alone, note which labelled breaker tripped, what was running and how often it happened, and share those notes with the contractor.

Does a finished basement change how a fault gets assessed?

It can. If your home has a finished or part-finished basement, the panel may be behind storage and cable routes may be hidden. Tell the contractor early so access to the panel and any concealed routes can be planned.

Who actually signs the contract once I submit these details?

The contractor you choose, not this website. An enquiry sent in is first reviewed; the site publishes information, with no hand in visits or pricing, nor in electrical work. A provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you may hire shows its licence and who it is separately before anything is agreed; their contract covers scope and price, then timing.
